When I was a hobby programmer I'd do my best to spend time crafting every part of my project and adding every neat feature I can think of.

When I got hired, I quickly learned that it's wrong.

What's the difference? Promises. Someone promised a deadline and you have to meet it because you also promised some payment come end of month.

To do so one must produce output as cheaply as possible. Least amount of resources that still meets the minimum standard.

You know you're improving when you can have a higher-than-average standard while producing work cheaply. That could mean, familiarity with code, communicating more efficiently, better tools, etc.


You know your company is making a lot of money when they can afford more expensive work - that means more time for development.

I would argue that this applies to most passion hobbies turned into jobs.
